I'm making a short film and I need you to write Suno music prompts for it. You already know how I want the prompts written, so follow those instructions. Here is everything about the film, the scenes made so far, and my taste in music.

THE FILM
Title: IRON SUNDAY. A 6-8 minute AI-generated sci-fi spaghetti western with creature characters, 16:9, made for YouTube. Fast, kinetic, very little dialogue, lots of action. I edit it myself and lay the music under the scenes.
Logline: "He never runs. He just never stops."

WORLD
An off-world desert frontier called The Parch: rust-red mesas, cracked dust, blinding white salt flats, bleached sky with two pale moons in daylight. No law. Towns are scrap settlements of rusty tin, pipes and warped planks. Everything is dusty, rusty and old. The characters are grotesque creatures (reptiles, insects, scorpions, hyenas, crocodiles) riding gaunt reptile horses and rusty hover-trucks. Gritty, stylish and a little comic. The violence is action-movie style, not gory.

HERO
THE ORGANIST. A very tall, thin, silent gunman. His face is hidden behind a riveted rusty steel mask, with a dark brown hat, a faded red scarf, a long sand-coloured duster and two sawn-off double-barrel shotguns. He never speaks and never runs. He walks while everyone else runs. He rides a black reptile horse. The gang thought they had buried him three years ago, and now he is back. Nobody knows who he really is.

VILLAINS
- CALDERA: a big gila-monster gang boss with gold teeth and a silver cross. He is cocky, laughs a lot, and dies at the end of the first scene.
- His gang: creature gunmen on reptile horses, plus two hover-trucks.
- THE VOICE: the real villain, never seen yet. He is a deep, gravelly boss heard only over radios. At first he is cold and amused. Later he gets angry and short-tempered as his men keep dying. He is the one who "put the Organist in the ground" once. The story is a chain: every man the Organist kills leads to the next one above him, climbing toward THE VOICE.

OVERALL THEME AND FEEL
A lone, unstoppable avenger working his way up a chain of command across the desert. The mood is cool, dangerous and heroic, with swagger and a bit of dark humour. It is not sad, not slow and not horror. Think of the energy of a classic spaghetti western mixed with a modern fast action edit.

SCENES MADE SO FAR (in film order, approximate lengths)
1. OPENING RIDE (about 75 s, credits run over it). Nine creature gunmen on reptile horses and two hover-trucks race flat out across the open desert in bright midday sun. We see individual riders, serious and focused, then the convoy reaches a lonely ruined church, the Iron Mission, and the gang walks to its doors. Big, epic and energetic. This introduces the film.
2. THE CHURCH (about 30 s). Inside the dark ruined church, the masked Organist sits playing a wheezing old pipe organ. The gang bursts in. Caldera puts his hand on the organ and the music stops: "You are in my church, friend." The Organist pulls two shotguns out of the organ and the first gunman goes flying. The tension snaps into action.
3. CHURCH GUNFIGHT AND YARD (about 30 s). Fast shotgun fight. The gunmen go down one after another. Caldera runs out the back door into the yard and is shot down beside his horse. The Organist stands alone with smoking barrels, and the film's title appears.
4. THE RADIO (about 30 s). A truck radio crackles. THE VOICE calls for Caldera, then realises who is listening: "I put you in the ground once, gunman. Keep riding... and I'll put you back." The Organist shoots the radio and gallops away. Mysterious and menacing, then a push forward.
5. TRAVEL MONTAGE (about 30 s). The Organist gallops at full speed through dust storms and scrap towns past strange creature townsfolk: a mantis trader, a beetle miner, an old tortoise woman, a little scorpion. He slows and stops at the edge of a huge white salt flat. Adventurous, fast and fun.
6. SALT FLATS AMBUSH (about 60 s). Out on the blinding salt, a hover-truck with a roof machine gun and a scorpion rider charge at him head-on. He charges straight back and shoots the rider off his horse, then the machine gunner off the roof. He dodges the truck at the last moment and blasts its thruster, and the truck flips and rolls. Big, loud, wild action.
7. AFTERMATH (about 30 s). Quiet on the salt, with smoke rising from the wreck and bodies lying still. The truck radio barks: THE VOICE is now angry, "Boys? Pick up!... Answer me, you dogs!", and he warns a radio tower that the Organist is coming. The Organist spots a far radio mast, mounts, and gallops toward it.
8. DISTANT TOWER (5 s). Only the top of a radio mast with a blinking red light, far away in the blowing dust.
